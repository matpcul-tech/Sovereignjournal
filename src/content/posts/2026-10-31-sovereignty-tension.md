The neocloud financing model is hitting its limit, and it's happening in public.

Debt markets have largely closed to pure-play GPU cloud providers without substantial non-AI revenue. CoreWeave, Nebius and others have seen sharp equity drops, bond yields drifting into distressed territory, and credit spreads pricing in real default risk. Gartner projects roughly 80% of independent neoclouds will lose their independence by 2030. On October 25, Elon Musk said more than 200 of them face consolidation because financing is no longer available without independent cash flow.

That is counterparty risk. It shows up the moment the entity running your models doesn't own the hardware.

A single containerized node, owned by the organization that uses the compute and holds the data, removes that exposure.

Capital is spent once. The asset stays on the owner's balance sheet. Governance (encryption, key control, audit) lives on hardware the owner controls.

One node at a time matches the financing reality now in plain view. Large leveraged builds are constrained. A single node can be funded and operated by the party that actually needs the AI.

The market data isn't theoretical anymore. The design that puts ownership with the user of the compute is already written.

Borrowed rails run fast. They just don't run yours.
