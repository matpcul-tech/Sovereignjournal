Congress just made it easier to site AI data centers. Critics say it's a giveaway to the industry. They're probably right about who benefits most.

This is exactly what Equity-Centered Flexibility, one of the four core constructs in Adaptive Inclusive Leadership Theory, was built to explain.

Flexibility is not neutral. When you reduce friction, you reduce it most for whoever already had momentum. A hyperscaler with a ten-billion-dollar balance sheet and a permitting team does not benefit from faster approvals at the same rate as a tribal nation siting its first compute node.

Making the race faster does not fix the starting lines. It locks them in.

AILT's response is not to oppose speed. It is a question of sequence: who was included in designing the flexibility before it was deployed?

If sovereign governments and rural communities had been in that room, "streamlined" might mean a parallel sovereign track, not just a faster lane inside a federal process built for private industry.

At Sovereign Shield, the node goes to the community. The data stays on the land. Governance gets designed by the people who live with the consequences.

That is Equity-Centered Flexibility applied to infrastructure. You build equity into the design before you build speed into it.
