# Voice guide for the journal

Written in Matt's voice, first person, from Ada, Oklahoma.

## What the voice is
- Direct. Short sentences carry the weight. Specifics do the narrative work.
- A practitioner writing theory, not a professor writing about practice. Every abstraction gets a concrete anchor: a node, a postal route, a casino floor, a clerk, a container.
- Confident without pitching. The reader should learn something, not be sold something.
- Willing to name a cost. Trade-offs get stated, not smoothed over.

## What the voice is not
- No hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, unlock, leverage, empower, journey, delve, tapestry, landscape.
- No throat-clearing openers ("In today's fast-paced world").
- No rhetorical question stacks. One question at most per piece.
- No listicle framing unless the content is a real sequence.
- No em dashes. Ever. Use a period, a comma, or a colon.

## Fixed language
- Always "41,000+ training pairs".
- Always "HIPAA-aligned", never "HIPAA compliant".
- Always "sovereign and rural communities", never "urban".
- Company one-liner: "the sovereign hyperscaler for nations and communities."
- Manifesto headline: "Building the Next Hyperscaler One Node at a Time."
- Tagline: "Pick up enough crumbs, you get a loaf of bread."
- Never "Power Capsules".
- Investor numbers, valuations, and named private contacts never appear in a post.

## Greeting protocol
- A piece may open with Halito and close with Yakoke or Chokma when it fits. Not every piece. Never forced.
